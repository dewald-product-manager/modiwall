The double volume is where a house shows what it thinks of itself. It is also where most of them run out of ideas and hang a very large lamp.

Why plants work here

Height needs something that is one thing from the floor and another from the landing. From below, a planted wall is a hillside. From the bridge upstairs you are among the leaves.

Sound

Glass, stone and plaster throw every footstep back. Foliage breaks it up. People usually notice the quiet before they can say what has changed.

From below it is a hillside. From the bridge you are among the leaves.

Light

Tall rooms usually have tall windows, which is half the work. The other half is the foot of the wall, where daylight arrives last. We plant it for shade, and light it if it needs it.

Reach

Ask this first. Will a small lift fit through the door? Can a tower stand on that floor? If not, we plan ropes, or a slim ledge, while the architect still has a pencil.

Where it stops

Give it an edge. A frame of slate, a steel reveal, a timber screen that ends where the leaves begin. Tall planting with a hard edge looks built. Without one it looks as though it got away.
