A good indoor wall is not a collection of rare plants. It is eight or ten species that like the same room, used generously, with one or two that make you look twice.

For a bright room, out of the sun

Beside a large window or under a skylight you can have colour. Golden philodendron for a lime that glows in winter. Marble queen and variegated umbrella for cream. Curly spider plant to break the edge. Variegation is paid for in light: the paler the leaf, the more of it the plant needs.

For an ordinary living room

This is where the all-rounders belong. Heart-leaf philodendron falls in curtains. Dwarf umbrella fills a wall with small green hands. Dracaena gives it height, and tree philodendron gives it drama. If you plant only these four, and plant them well, people will still stop.

For a darker corner

Peace lily, compact dracaena, Boston fern and the deep green philodendrons. Dark leaves are dark because they have learnt to live on little. Put them where the lamp reaches and they will look better at night than they do by day.

Plant in drifts

One of everything looks like a nursery. A drift of forty philodendron with a seam of fern through it looks like a hillside. We set each planting out as one composition for morning, midday or evening light, and you can see all three on the wall before you choose.

If there are animals in the house

Tell us. Philodendron, pothos, dracaena, umbrella and peace lily can make a cat or dog unwell if it chews them. Spider plant, peperomia and Boston fern are gentler company, and a wall can start above the reach of a curious mouth.

And the law

Two favourites carry conditions in South Africa. Wild-type sword fern is a listed invasive; the sterile Boston cultivars are not. Dwarf umbrella is listed in the Eastern Cape, KwaZulu-Natal, Limpopo and Mpumalanga. Ask any installer what they plant, and where.
