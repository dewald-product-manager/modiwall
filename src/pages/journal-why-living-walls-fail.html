A living wall rarely dies of one thing. It dies of something small that nobody was watching.

The water stops

A blocked dripper, a tripped timer, a tap turned off by a guest. In many systems the top dries while the bottom drowns. Ask how water reaches the plant in the furthest corner. In a Modiwall it runs through channels inside the panels, each feeding the one beneath, and returns to be used again.

The plants were chosen for the photograph

A sun lover in a lobby looks splendid for six weeks. Light decides the planting. Taste comes second.

One plant takes its neighbours with it

Where roots share a felt or a trough, removing one tears the next. Where each has its own vessel, one is lifted out, another takes its place, and the rest never know.

Nobody came back

This is the commonest of all. A wall is a garden, and no garden thrives on the day it was planted alone. Ours are tended weekly or monthly.

What the research says

A 2024 University of Pretoria study, testing two systems outdoors for six months, favoured locally produced modular walls made from few, robust, recycled components. It names Modiwall among South Africa’s local systems, though Modiwall was not one of the two it tested.
