A wall is not a bunch of flowers, and it is not a sculpture either. It is a garden, which means the question has a gardener’s answer: as long as somebody looks after it.

One wall, nearly six years

In May 2019 we planted a wall for Hollard, framed in slate. We photographed it that August, when it had closed over; again in 2022, and in 2023; and in February 2025. It is fuller in the last picture than in the first. You can move between the years yourself.

The plants

Some will be there in five years. Some will tire in two. Neither is a fault. In a Modiwall a single vessel is lifted out and another takes its place, so the wall is renewed the way skin is, without anybody seeing it happen.

It is fuller in the last picture than in the first.

The bones

The panels, grid and vessels are moulded in UV-resistant recycled polypropylene, and the reservoir is steel. There is no felt to rot and no timber to swell.

The moving parts

A pump runs for a few minutes a day and will one day stop. A timer has a battery. Both are small, both are ours to look after, and neither should be allowed to surprise you.

What shortens it

Too little light. A tap closed by someone helpful. A contract that ended, and nobody noticed. We have written about the ways walls fail, and none of them is old age.

Before you commission one, from anybody, ask to see a wall they planted five years ago. Ask us too.
