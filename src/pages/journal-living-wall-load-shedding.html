South Africans ask this before they ask the price. It is a fair question to put to anything with a pump.

Minutes, not hours

A wall does not need power the way a fridge does. The pump runs for a few minutes at a time, usually twice a day in summer. For the rest of the day the wall is simply a garden, with the lights off.

The soil is the battery

Every plant stands in its own vessel of soil, close to a litre of it. That soil is the reserve. Miss one watering and nothing happens. Systems that grow in felt, with bare roots and no soil, have no such patience, and it is worth asking which you are being sold.

Miss one watering and nothing happens.

The timer remembers

The digital timer holds its programme on a battery of its own. When the power returns the wall carries on from the right hour, without anybody resetting a clock behind a plant.

For longer outages

If your area loses power for days, put the pump on the circuit your inverter already protects. It is a small, short load. In a holiday house, tell us when it stands empty and we will set the wall, and our visits, to suit.

And the light

Walls that live on daylight do not care. Walls under grow lights lose a few hours of day, which a forest plant treats as weather. It is the weeks that matter, not the evening.
