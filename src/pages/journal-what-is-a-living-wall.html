Say vertical garden to a landscaper and living wall to an architect and they will picture the same thing. The differences worth knowing are underneath.

A green facade climbs

Ivy on an old house is a green facade. The plants are rooted in the ground and the wall is only what they hold on to. It is inexpensive, it takes years, and it decides for itself where it stops.

A living wall is planted

Here the plants are rooted in the wall, at every height. It is full on the day it is finished. It can be composed, like a border. And because roots are up there, so must be water, soil and a way to reach them.

Felt, trays or vessels

Some systems keep plants in a wet felt. Some use troughs. In a Modiwall every plant has its own vessel of soil, hooked on to a grid in front of a panel that carries the water. One plant can be changed without its neighbours knowing.

It is full on the day it is finished.

And artificial green walls

These are made, not grown. For a windowless corridor or a ceiling nobody can reach they are the honest choice, and we make those too. They should never be sold as the same thing.

Which one is yours?

If you have a bare boundary and ten years, plant a climber. If you have a wall you look at every day, and would like it to be a garden by next month, that is a living wall. Photograph it and see.
