It is the most repeated claim in our trade. Plants scrub the air. Put enough of them on a wall and the room is cleaner. It is a lovely idea, and it does not hold.

Where it came from

Laboratories sealed plants into small chambers, added a pollutant and watched the level fall. It did fall. A chamber, though, has no windows, no doors and nobody walking through.

What happened next

Two engineers at Drexel University gathered a dozen of those studies and converted the results to the rate used for air cleaners. To match the air that already moves in and out of an ordinary building, you would need between ten and a thousand plants for every square metre of floor.

To match an ordinary building’s air, you would need up to a thousand plants on every square metre of floor.

Outdoors too

A six-month study of green walls in a city centre found no significant difference in particulates beside them. It did find significantly less noise.

Why we plant them anyway

Because people work better near plants. Because time with plants appears to lower stress. Because a wall that grows is beautiful in a way nothing else in the room can be. That is where the evidence is, and it is reason enough.
