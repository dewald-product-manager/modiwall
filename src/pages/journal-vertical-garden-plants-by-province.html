South Africa is one country on a map and five to a plant. The national botanical institute, SANBI, divides it into horticultural zones by two things: when the rain comes, and whether it freezes. A wall garden feels both sooner than a border does, because its roots are up in the air.

Summer rain, no frost

The KwaZulu-Natal coast and the Eastern Cape as far as Gqeberha. Winters are dry and mild. Spurflower, asparagus fern and hen and chickens are in their element. Near the sea, plan for wind and salt before you plan for anything else.

Winter rain, no frost

The Western Cape coast. The difficulty is February, not July. Choose silver leaves and succulents that can wait: helichrysum, pig’s ear, crassula, spekboom.

Karoo

Dry, bright, cold at night. Plants that store water, and grasses. The wall is watered from inside, so what matters is sun and frost rather than rain.

Highveld and the interior

Gauteng, the Free State, the Mpumalanga Highveld. Storms in summer and frost in winter. Blue fescue, stonecrop, bulbine and lily turf come through it. Purple heart and spurflower want a wall that faces north or stands under a roof.

Bushveld and Lowveld

Limpopo, much of North West, the Mpumalanga Lowveld. Hot, with light frost if any. Sun planting is easy. Under a roof you can have almost a rainforest.

Then the law

Some garden plants are listed as invasive in some provinces and not in others. It is one more reason that a plant list from Cape Town is not the right list for Nelspruit.
