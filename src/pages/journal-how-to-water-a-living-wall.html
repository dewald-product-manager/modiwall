People expect a living wall to be thirsty. It is closer to the truth to say that it is punctual. A little, often, in the right place, and then nothing.

Down the inside

Water enters along the top, through a pipe hidden behind the planting. It runs down channels on the inside of the panels, never over the face. The foot of each panel seats in the head of the one below, so the wall feeds itself from top to bottom without a joint to leak. You can follow it through a section of the wall.

Through a wick

Each vessel carries a braided wick that passes through its hook into the channel. Capillary action does the rest. The soil draws what it can hold and then stops, which is why the plant at the bottom is not drowning while the one at the top goes without.

And back again

Whatever is left falls into a reservoir at the foot of the wall, usually steel, and a pump returns it to the top. Nothing runs down the paving. Nothing is lost except what the plants breathe out, and that is the part you will feel on a hot afternoon.

It is closer to the truth to say that it is punctual.

How much, how often

In the heart of summer, five to ten minutes in the morning and again in the afternoon. Indoors and in winter, less. A timer, set to the second, remembers for you. The right setting depends on the light, the wind and the planting, so we change it as the seasons do.

What you do

Very little. Walls that we keep have their levels, filters and feed checked on every visit. Watch for one thing only: a wall that has changed colour in one corner is telling you something. Call before it finishes the sentence.
