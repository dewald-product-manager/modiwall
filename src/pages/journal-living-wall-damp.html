Damp is not caused by plants. It is caused by water in the wrong place for long enough. So the useful question is not whether a wall has water in it, but where the water is.

Where the water is

In some systems the plants root into a felt, and the felt is kept wet. Everything behind it lives with that decision. In a Modiwall the water runs in a closed channel inside a moulded panel, and the soil sits in separate vessels in front. The face is dry. You can lean on it in linen.

What stands between

We still waterproof. A membrane goes up before the first panel does, and it is carried past the edges and down to the reservoir. It will probably never be needed. That is how waterproofing should be.

The face is dry. You can lean on it in linen.

And the air?

Plants release a little moisture as they grow. In a dry Highveld winter, with the heating on, most people would pay for that. In a small closed room we look at ventilation the way we would for a bathroom.

What to ask any installer

  • Where does the water run, and can it reach the wall behind?
  • What is the waterproofing, and who guarantees it?
  • What happens if the pump fails to stop?
  • Can a panel be opened to look behind it?

Good answers are short. If you are building, put the wall on the drawing early, when all of this costs least. If the house is already standing, send a photograph of the wall and we will tell you what is behind it that matters.
