Look at the roof first

A covered patio has bright shade, which is what most beautiful foliage would ask for. Near the open edge you can use bulbine and fescue. Deeper in, plectranthus, asparagus fern and the philodendrons you thought were houseplants.

Wind

On a balcony it is wind, not sun, that does the damage. It dries a leaf faster than heat. Small, tough, close-growing plants take the exposed end and the softer ones stand in their lee, as they would on a hillside.

Water that stays where it is put

A wall that keeps its own water needs no drain, stains no paving, and drips on no neighbour. In a sectional title that last point is often the one the trustees want in writing.

The braai

Keep the planting well away from flame and out of the path of the chimney. Beyond that the two are old friends. A wall of cool green takes the glare off white plaster and brings the temperature of a courtyard down to something you can sit in.

Light it from above

One row of warm lights, close to the wall, aimed down. Lit from above, every leaf throws a shadow on the next, and the garden is there after dark when you are.
