What plants on a wall do

Green walls attract big claims. Here is what the research supports, and where it does not.

The evidence

People work better near plants

Three field experiments in large commercial offices in the Netherlands and the UK measured concentration, satisfaction and productivity. Outcomes improved in all three when plants were added.

Nieuwenhuis, Knight, Postmes and Haslam, 2014, Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied. Read the study

Green walls are quieter

Researchers recorded noise at 12 green walls and 12 bare walls in a dense city centre for six months. It was significantly lower at the green walls.

Paull, Krix, Torpy and Irga, 2020, Int. J. Environmental Research and Public Health. Read the study

They shade the wall behind them

Reviews report cooler wall surfaces behind planting in daylight. How much depends on climate, plant cover and the air gap, so we do not quote one number for every building.

Cardinali, Balderrama, Arztmann and Pottgiesser, 2023, Nature-Based Solutions, “Green walls and health: An umbrella review”.

They do not clean a city’s air

The same 12-wall study found no significant difference in particulates or air temperature beside the walls. A living wall is worth having. It is not a filter for traffic.

Paull and others, 2020, as above.

Where a wall earns its place

Homes and estates

Bathrooms, stairwells, pool walls, patios and courtyards where there is no room for a bed.

Healthcare

Hospital atriums and waiting rooms, where a view of something growing is welcome.

Offices and reception

A first impression that softens a hard room and takes very little floor space.

Hospitality and retail

Lobbies, terraces and stands people photograph. We have planted a billboard.

Buildings

Planting hides an unsightly wall and shields the finish behind it from sun and weather.
