An outdoor wall is a cliff. It drains fast, it bakes, the wind finds it, and a pale wall opposite throws the sun back a second time. South Africa happens to be full of plants that evolved on cliffs.

For full sun

Bulbine, for yellow flowers most of the year. Spekboom, which shapes itself. Campfire crassula, which turns coral in winter light. Stonecrop for a lime carpet, and pig’s ear for a cool silver leaf the size of a spoon. These are not second-best plants for difficult places. They are the palette.

For morning sun and afternoon shade

The kindest aspect of all. Silver helichrysum, blue fescue, hen and chickens and purple heart, with liriope to hold the line between them.

For shade

South walls and covered terraces belong to plectranthus, asparagus fern and liriope. They are at their best when the rest of the garden is at its hottest, which is when you will be sitting there.

Indigenous first

Most of this list is from here, and that is deliberate. Sword fern and two of the wandering jews are listed as invasive, and Durban advises against purple heart on its coast, so a good outdoor list changes with the province.

Frost, and the coast

On the Highveld we keep tender species off exposed corners, and tell you plainly when one will not last where you want it. At the coast the question is salt, and succulents answer most of it.

Three outdoor mixes

We compose them as three: Karoo in coral and lime, Silver in silver and blue, Violet in purple and deep green. See them grown on a wall. The final list is always drawn for your own site, after we have stood in front of it at the hottest hour.

The wall itself is moulded in UV-resistant polypropylene and watered from inside, so the sun has less to say about it than it thinks. This is how it is made.
