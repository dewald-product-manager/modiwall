Most indoor walls fail in the dark, slowly, over a season. The cure is to measure first and plant second.

The numbers

University horticulturists in the United States put low-light foliage at about 50 to 250 foot-candles, and medium-light plants at 250 to 1,000. In the units your phone shows, that is roughly 540 to 2,700 lux, and 2,700 to 10,800 lux.

Measure it yourself

Install a light meter app. Hold the phone flat against the wall, screen facing out into the room, at the height the plants will be. Take a reading mid-morning, at noon and in mid-afternoon. Do it at the top and the bottom of the wall. The two are often very different.

Measure the wall, never the room. They are rarely the same.

Plant for what you find

Many of the plants we love best come from forest floors. Heart-leaf philodendron, ferns and the delicious monster evolved to make a living from what slips through the canopy. The gold-awarded atrium at Netcare Pinehaven relies on indirect natural light.

When it is too dark

Grow lights can be discreet, and warm enough to live with. If they are unwelcome, an artificial wall will always look better than a struggling one.
