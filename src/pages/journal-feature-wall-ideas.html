Every room has one wall that matters. It is the one you face when you come in, or sit opposite for twenty years. Four good things can go on it.

Stone

Permanent, cool, and superb with a light grazing it. It is also the same on the last day as on the first. Choose it where you want a room to stand still.

Timber

Warm, and kind to voices. Slats hide doors, televisions and other embarrassments. It will fade where the sun falls, and it will look better for it if you chose well.

Art

The only one of the four that can hold an opinion. A great painting needs empty wall around it, and it needs the sun kept off. Give it both, or give it another room.

A living wall

It moves when a door opens. It is lime in the morning and almost black under a lamp. It breaks up the hard echo of a double volume. It also has to be looked after, and anyone who says otherwise has not kept one. We look after ours.

Better together

The best rooms we have worked in use two. Planting framed in slate. A slatted screen that stops where the leaves begin. Marble in a bathroom, with a garden behind the glass. One material gives the other its edge.

Where it goes

  • Behind the dining table, where people sit longest.
  • At the end of a passage, so that the walk has a reason.
  • In a double-volume stair, seen from both floors.
  • Beside the bath.
  • Facing the front door.

Choose the wall first. Then photograph it and see it planted, in the light it really has.
