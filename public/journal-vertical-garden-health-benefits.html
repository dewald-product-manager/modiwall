What a living wall does for you

Greenery changes how a room feels, and the research is catching up with what gardeners have always known.

Words by Charl, edited for this journal. 2 September 2025. 2 minute read.

In a world where urban living is on the rise, the charm of a vertical garden is hard to ignore. Imagine walking into your home or office and being met by a wall of living green. It is beautiful first. It also does you good.

A calmer room

Time around plants appears to settle us. A randomised crossover study in 2015 found that interacting with indoor plants may reduce psychological and physiological stress in young adults. Imagine unwinding in front of a thriving wall after a long day. It can work wonders for your mood.

A clearer head

Three field experiments in large commercial offices in the Netherlands and the United Kingdom compared bare workspaces with the same workspaces furnished with plants. Concentration, satisfaction and productivity improved when the plants arrived.

It is beautiful first. It also does you good.

A quieter corner

Leaves and growing medium soften sound. Over six months, researchers recorded noise at twelve green walls and twelve bare ones in a dense city centre. It was significantly lower at the green walls.

A connection to nature

Many of us feel a disconnect from the natural world. In a well-known 1984 study, surgical patients whose window looked onto trees left hospital sooner, and needed fewer strong painkillers, than those who looked onto a brick wall. A view of something growing matters, and a living wall brings that view indoors.

What it will not do

A living wall will not replace an open window. Plants do not clean the air of a room in any useful quantity, and we would rather say so. They do release moisture as they breathe, which is welcome in a dry interior.

As towns grow denser, a planted wall is one of the few ways to give a room back its garden. Walk through some of ours.
